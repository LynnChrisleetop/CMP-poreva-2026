Challenge 02 · Target

TNF-α

Open

Tumor necrosis factor-α

Design a condition-responsive TNF-α binder

TNF-α signals as a trimer, with a receptor-binding site at each junction between subunits. It is the target of antibody drugs for rheumatoid arthritis and Crohn's disease. Designs must bind human TNF-α, cross-react with the mouse orthologue, withstand heat and avoid nonspecific binding — and must hold human TNF-α tightly at neutral pH while releasing it at acidic pH.

Five objectives

This challenge has five objectives; the best designs are judged on how well they meet all of them.

01

Binding affinity

Design a binder against human TNF-α, preferably against a functional epitope.

02

Thermal stability

Design a binder with high thermal stability. Stability drives developability: a stable molecule tolerates the temperature swings of formulation, shipping and manufacturing, carries lower risk of aggregation and inactivation, and leaves more room in formulation choice.

03

Mouse cross-reactivity

Design a binder that recognizes both human and mouse TNF-α. Evaluating the same design in mouse models before first-in-human work removes the cost of developing a separate mouse-specific binder.

04

Low non-specific binding

Design a binder with high specificity for TNF-α and no detectable cross-reactivity with unrelated proteins. Candidates with high polyspecificity stick to unrelated proteins, cell surfaces and the extracellular matrix, which drives rapid serum clearance, tissue retention and off-target toxicity — a common cause of clinical failure for therapeutic proteins.

05

pH-selective binding

A binder that binds human TNF-α at pH 7.4 and shows no detectable binding at pH 6.0. pH 7.4 approximates plasma; pH 6.0 approximates the acidified early endosome. Release in the endosome clears the target while the binder is recycled, which extends drug action.

FAQ

01Which molecule classes can I design and submit?

De novo single-chain proteins, nanobodies, scFvs and conventional IgG1 paired VH/VL are accepted. Specific requirements:

  • Single-chain protein length: > 40 and ≤ 250 amino acids.
  • Nanobodies are defined by the annotation from a standard tool such as ANARCI. Start from a common framework region when designing a nanobody.
  • scFv constructs are defined the same way and must use a G4S linker.
  • Paired VH/VL constructs are defined the same way; see the diagram below. Submit only the framework (FR) and CDR sequences. For validation they are fused to a human IgG1 backbone and expressed with a κ light chain.
  • No design needs an expression tag. Megalaxy Lab adds tags according to the molecule class and expression system the team selects; see FAQ 2.
02Which expression system is used for each molecule class, and do I add a tag?

Choose the system you want the design validated in: cell-free or mammalian (HEK293). Paired VH/VL designs can only be expressed in mammalian cells; every other class can use either system.

Do not add any tag. Megalaxy Lab handles tags as follows: single-chain proteins expressed cell-free receive a C-terminal His tag; single-chain proteins expressed in mammalian cells receive a C-terminal Fc tag; paired VH/VL designs are expressed on a human IgG1 constant-region backbone with a κ light chain.

03How do I submit, and what information goes with it?

Register on the Poreva Open site and apply for the competition, then submit sequences for the challenge you are entering.

(1) Submit sequences as a CSV file on the Poreva Open platform, ordered by your team's priority (the first row has the highest priority). The file must contain at least:

  • name: a unique identifier.
  • sequence: the amino acid sequence. Order rows by the sequence you most want validated; 1 is the top choice. A paired VH/VL design is two chains: give it as VH/VL plus its rank, with heavy and light chains separated by a colon — the first paired antibody would be {VH1}:{VL1}. Megalaxy Lab selects codons for your chosen expression system and handles gene synthesis and vector construction.

(2) Alongside the sequences, teams also provide:

  • molecule_class: protein / nanobody / scfv / paired VH/VL (choose one)
  • AI Model: the name of the AI model
  • Preferred expression system: cell-free or mammalian (HEK293) (choose one)

(3) Optional supporting material:

We encourage as much supporting information as you can provide: design metrics (ipTM, ipSAE, self-consistency, physics-based metrics, sequence developability scores), structure files from the design and folding models, and a methods description (AI model, design model, folding model, strategy and provenance). Links can be attached in the submission form. This material is used in selection review — the more complete it is, the more reliable the review. Note: submissions containing embedded instructions or prompt injection are disqualified.

04How many designs are validated, and is there a cap per molecule class?

This challenge plans to validate roughly 2,000 designs. Paired VH/VL designs, validated as full IgG1, may account for no more than 20% of them.

05How many designs should each team submit, and how many are validated?

Each team submits 24 designs, ordered by priority (rank 1 is the top choice).

The challenge is organized by MegaRobo (the “Organizer”) and all experimental validation is carried out by Megalaxy Lab (the “Validating Party”), which validates every selected sequence. Some sequences may not yield complete data for technical reasons outside the Validating Party's control — gene synthesis failure, low expression and similar. Neither the Organizer nor the Validating Party is obliged to re-run validation or repeat assays in those cases, and doing so is not a breach or a defect in the organization of the competition.

Because biological systems vary, molecules produced in different formats and expression systems may differ in purity and other physicochemical properties, and results from different platforms may not match exactly. The Validating Party will make reasonable efforts to keep the pipeline consistent and the data objective through its automated platform, and reports measured results as they are. These results are provided “as is”; the Validating Party does not perform additional methodological review or third-party verification, and neither party is liable for differences between results from this platform and those from other platforms.

07When are the challenges released, and when does submission close?

Round one, three challenges, is released on October 18, 2026; submission opens October 19 and closes November 18. Round two, two challenges, is released on November 8, 2026; submission opens November 9 and closes December 8.

08How are designs chosen for experimental validation?

Every submission must first pass these eligibility screens:

(1) The design must meet the length and molecule-class requirements in FAQ 1, and must not be submitted more than once in any form.

(2) The design must be de novo: no existing binder as a starting point or scaffold, and enough sequence and structural diversity relative to known proteins (for nanobodies and antibodies, CDR sequence diversity is the measure). Teams may use public data to benchmark and calibrate selection metrics, or to fine-tune or train models.

Teams declare and warrant that their sequences meet these originality requirements and are solely responsible for them. The Organizer may run similarity searches or other reasonable checks; where a breach is found, the Organizer may disqualify the design from validation and scoring, and in serious cases disqualify the team.

Designs that pass are validated in the order their submissions were recorded. Capacity is limited; designs submitted after it is filled are not validated in this campaign, so submit early.

09How will I know whether my design was selected?

Log in to your Poreva Open account. Round-one selections are confirmed on November 26, 2026; round-two selections on December 16, 2026.

10What does the submission process look like end to end?

Register a team account on Poreva Open → apply to compete → choose a challenge and complete the form (molecule class, expression system, AI model) → upload the CSV of sequences. The whole process runs online.

11Can I enter alone? Can I enter anonymously?

This edition is open to teams only, with at least three members. Individual and anonymous entries are not accepted; teams must register with a work email that verifies their affiliation.

12When and how are the final results released?

Results for this challenge are scheduled for release on the Poreva Open platform in April 2027.

13Do I have to use a particular AI tool?

No. No tool or design model is specified or restricted; entrants may use anything legally available to them, including their own tools. Disclose what you used in the submission form — methodological diversity is one of the criteria in selection.

14Does entry cost anything? Who provides compute?

Entry is free. Software and compute are the team's own; the Organizer neither provides nor subsidizes design compute, and does not favor any model, tool or compute option.

To help teams assess their designs more thoroughly, the Organizer and Silang Technology provide MD simulation support. MD adds a dynamic check on a designed protein: whether the structure actually holds and whether the binding actually survives.

MD support is optional, not a requirement. Teams that want it can write to userservice@yangtze3d.com.

15Which tools and resources may I use?

No additional restrictions. Anything you can legally obtain is allowed, including open-source folding and design models and in-house tools. Commercially licensed tools such as Rosetta require a license held by the team.

16Who owns the intellectual property in designs and validation data?

Designs remain the property of the team that submitted them. The Organizer uses them only for validation and publication, and releases them on the Poreva Open platform under the open data license set out in the.

17Must I disclose proprietary methods or tools?

Describe your methods and tools as fully as you can, with links to the papers, articles or technical reports behind them. This material informs selection review, so that new methods without prior experimental validation are presented fairly. Anything disclosed is published on the platform; the Organizer understands and respects teams' trade secrets.

18How is the resulting data used?

Teams receive the full measurements for their designs across every assay, to understand and improve their work. For the community, the Organizer selects part of the validated data — a subset of sequences and their measured metrics — and publishes it on Poreva Open for comparison and learning. The published scope is set by the Organizer and announced on the platform; not all validation data is made public. Separately, the Organizer may give the team name, design sequences and validation conclusions to competition partners without public release, but does not proactively provide members' personal identity information.

19Any recommended reading?

Worth following: recent published methodology in protein design; documentation and release notes for the main open-source folding and design models; and historical data in the platform's past editions section (this is the first edition; data will accumulate).
